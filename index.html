Methodology

Built on documented OR theory.
Not heuristics. Not guesses.

01
Takt-Time Demand Derivation
Operator demand is computed from first principles: Ops = ⌈Work_Content / Takt⌉ where Takt = Net Available Time ÷ Daily Demand. Evening and night ratios (0.75, 0.55) follow Ernst et al. (2004 EJOR).
Womack & Jones 1996
02
Dantzig Set-Covering MILP
One sub-problem per station-shift: minimize total workers assigned to 5-on-2-off patterns while covering demand every day of the week. Totally unimodular matrix guarantees LP relaxation is integer-optimal.
Dantzig OR 2(3) 1954
03
Post-Solve Validation
After every solve, all(A @ x ≥ demand_vec) is checked explicitly for all 7 days. The UI shows a YES/NO column — not an assumed pass. Solver: HiGHS via scipy.optimize.milp.
Veinott & Wagner 1962
04
Loaded Labor Cost Model
Loaded cost = base wage × hours × 1.30 burden rate (SHRM 2023 US manufacturing benchmark). Contractor bill rate includes 1.15 agency markup. Mix sensitivity table shows exact weekly cost per FT/PT/CT ratio.
SHRM Benefits Survey 2023
05
ISO 22400-2 OEE
OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality, computed from user-supplied inputs per the international standard. Not predicted from headcount — OEE depends on equipment state, not staffing alone.
ISO 22400-2:2014
06
AI Schedule Debrief
After each run, Groq (Llama 3.3 70B) generates a structured debrief with coverage flags, cost concentration, night-shift proportionality, and one action item — using only MILP output numbers, no generic advice.
Groq · Llama 3.3 70B

Model Formulation

The math, not the marketing.

// Demand Derivation // Womack & Jones (1996) takt time T = Net_Available / Demand operators per station Ops_day = ⌈ WC / T ⌉ Ops_eve = ⌈ Ops_day × 0.75 ⌉ Ops_ngt = ⌈ Ops_day × 0.55 ⌉ Weekend = ⌈ Weekday × 0.85 ⌉ // Loaded Cost // SHRM 2023 FT = wage × hrs × 1.30 CT = wage × hrs × 1.30 × 1.15 // OEE — ISO 22400-2:2014 OEE = A × P × Q
